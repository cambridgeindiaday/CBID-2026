General Information

Why is Cambridge India Day needed in Cambridge?

Cambridge is home to a large and growing Indian community, including professionals, researchers, students, entrepreneurs, doctors, healthcare workers, academics, and families. Many have made Cambridge their home and contribute significantly to the city's economic, educational, scientific, and cultural life.

Despite this strong presence, there has been no large-scale public event dedicated to India, showcasing the diversity, heritage, achievements, and contributions of India and the Indian diaspora in Cambridge.

Indian communities have been celebrating their festivals and traditions in Cambridgeshire for many years. These celebrations are often organised indoors in village halls, community centres, schools, religious venues, and parish halls. Many of these events naturally focus on a particular region, language group, state, faith, or cultural community.

Cambridge India Day was created to fill that gap. The vision is simple: All States, Many Languages, Many Cultures, One Indian Diaspora.

Cambridge India Day provides something different. It brings together people from across India, from North, South, East, West, and North-East India, on a single public platform. The event works with communities connected to all 28 States and 8 Union Territories, showcasing the diversity, traditions, heritage, food, arts, music, dance, and achievements of the nation, and promotes friendship, mutual respect, community cohesion, and cultural understanding among all who live in Cambridge and Cambridgeshire.

At the same time, Cambridge India Day serves as a cultural bridge between India and the wider global community. It provides an opportunity for local British residents, international visitors, academics, students, and people from all backgrounds to learn about and experience the richness of Indian civilisation and its diverse traditions.

Through its dance, music, food, attire, languages, arts, crafts, artefacts, literature, and linguistic heritage, Cambridge India Day promotes cultural understanding, education, and appreciation. Visitors can explore the traditions of different Indian states and union territories, discover regional customs and histories, and gain a deeper understanding of one of the world's oldest and most diverse civilisations.

By bringing these cultural expressions into a public space, Cambridge India Day creates opportunities for dialogue, friendship, learning, and mutual respect between communities. It is not only a celebration for the Indian diaspora but also an educational and cultural experience for the wider Cambridge community and the international population that calls Cambridge home.

How did the idea of Cambridge India Day originate? What is the history behind its creation?

The idea of Cambridge India Day was conceived by a charity, CamCareUK, which has a long track record of delivering community cohesion, cultural, educational, and wellbeing projects across Cambridgeshire.

The inspiration came from the events organised by the High Commission of India in London, where all Indian states exhibited, projecting arts, crafts, and heritage, ran the food festival where each state offered the state's delicacy free to the visitors, and the artists projected their states via folk and classical performances.

CamCareUK realised there was no equivalent public event in Cambridge that could showcase India on such a scale, and the concept was born in 2023.

Recognising this gap, Dr. Bhattacharya from CamCareUK envisioned an event that would bring together all Indian communities, regardless of state, language, religion, ethnicity, or cultural background, under one banner.

The aim was to create a free, inclusive, and highly visible celebration in the heart of Cambridge, where both the Indian diaspora and the wider public could come together to experience the richness of India's presence in Cambridge.

Today, Cambridge India Day serves not only as a celebration of Indian heritage but also as a cultural bridge between India, Cambridge, and the wider international community that calls the city home.

Which was the first year of Cambridge India Day?

The concept of Cambridge India Day was first introduced in 2024, when CamCare UK organised a pilot event at Christ's Pieces Park in Cambridge City Centre.

The pilot events held in 2024 and 2025 helped test the concept, engage with the Indian community, build partnerships, and demonstrate public interest in a large-scale celebration of Indian culture in Cambridge.

What was the format of Cambridge India Day in 2024 and 2025?

In the first years, in 2024 and 2025, the emphasis was on State Exhibitions, State Performances, Regional Local Food, Indian Dhol, and a Fashion Parade.

What is the format of Cambridge India Day in 2026?

Cambridge India Day is a free two-day community festival, on the first weekend in July in Cambridge, showcasing culture, traditions, food, music, dance, diversity, travel, tourism, language, literature, and heritage of all 28 states of India in the heart of Cambridge.

State Pavilions & Exhibitions

Why are there State Pavilions and Exhibitions?
  • India is a remarkably diverse country with different official languages, traditions, food, clothing, music, dance, art, architecture, and cultural heritage, a very similar situation to mainland Europe.
  • One of the important objectives of the State Pavilions is education. Many children from Indian families who are born and brought up in Cambridge and across the UK often have limited opportunities to learn about the wider diversity of India beyond their family's home state or the places where their grandparents live.
  • The State Pavilions provide an opportunity for them to discover the history, geography, languages, festivals, food, clothing, and cultural heritage of other parts of India. In this way, they can gain a broader understanding of the country from which their families originate.
  • The exhibitions also benefit local British residents who may never have visited India, offering them a unique opportunity to explore India's diverse cultures without travelling thousands of miles. The same applies to the international and global communities living in Cambridge, many of whom are keen to learn more about India and its people.
  • Through these exhibitions, Cambridge India Day becomes not only a cultural celebration but also an educational experience, helping to build understanding, appreciation, and connections between communities.
How can my state participate?

Community members of a State can organise a pavilion representing their state through displays, costumes, arts, crafts, food, tourism, and cultural activities.

The name of the Pavilion has to be named after the State of India, for example Assam, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Kerala, Odisha, and not by the name of any local communities.

Can more than one organisation represent a state?

Yes. Collaboration is encouraged in Cambridge India Day.

For example: Five different community groups of a particular state can represent their state together.

Performing

Can I perform at Cambridge India Day?

Yes. Dance schools, music groups, cultural organisations, and the state representative performers are welcome to perform.

Is there a fee for performers?

No. Participation is free, subject to stage slot availability.

How long is each performance slot?

Performance times vary depending on the type of programme:

  • State Associations are allocated up to 10 minutes each to showcase their state's culture, music, dance, traditions, or heritage.
  • Professional dance schools and music schools are allocated between 15 and 30 minutes.

The final schedule is determined by the Cultural Committee to ensure that all State groups and the schools have a fair opportunity to perform while maintaining a varied and engaging programme throughout the event.
